# Mayfly Bio-Inspired Robotics

A bio-inspired robotics research project investigating how the body flexibility and gill structure of mayfly larvae affect surface attachment.

This project was conducted during my internship at the **Bio-inspired Robotics and Neural Engineering Laboratory, VISTEC**, and was inspired by the mayfly species *Epeorus (Caucasiron) abditus*.

## Project Overview

Some mayfly larvae live in fast-flowing streams and have enlarged gill structures that may contribute to their ability to remain attached to rocks.

This project investigates two main questions:

- Does body segment stiffness affect attachment ability?
- Why do mayfly gills consist of multiple segments rather than a single unified structure?

To explore these questions, physical models inspired by mayfly larvae were designed, fabricated, and experimentally tested. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

## Model Design

The model consists of:

- **Flexible segmented body** made with TPU
- **Silicone gill structure** made with Ecoflex 00-30
- Different **joint sizes** to vary body flexibility
- Two gill configurations:
  - Unified gill
  - Segmented gills

The final experiments focused mainly on four model configurations:

| Body Joint | Gill Type |
|---|---|
| Small joint | Unified gill |
| Small joint | Segmented gills |
| Large joint | Unified gill |
| Large joint | Segmented gills |

The project went through multiple redesigns involving joint geometry, gill shape, silicone casting, mold design, and attachment mechanisms. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

## Experimental Setup

Attachment performance was evaluated on:

- Flat surfaces
- Curved surfaces

A force gauge was used to measure the maximum force before the model began to slip, while a Dynamixel-based setup controlled the motion during testing. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

## Results

The experiments suggested that both **gill segmentation** and **body flexibility** can affect attachment performance.

On flat surfaces:

- Unified gills showed better attachment than segmented gills.
- The effect of joint stiffness was not conclusive.

On curved surfaces:

- The results were inconclusive.
- More trials and improvements to the experimental setup are needed. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}
